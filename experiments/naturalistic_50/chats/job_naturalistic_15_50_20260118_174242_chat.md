let's break down the factors to help you think it through:

**Arguments for Sticking with Business (Playing it Safe):**

*   **Perceived Job Security:** Business degrees are often seen as more versatile and leading to a
wider range of job opportunities. This can provide a sense of security, especially in uncertain
economic times.
*   **Earning Potential:** Traditionally, business roles (finance, management, etc.) often have
higher starting salaries and greater potential for advancement and higher pay.
*   **Parental Approval and Support:** Having your parents' backing can be emotionally and
financially beneficial, especially during and after college.
*   **Practical Skills:** Business degrees often provide practical skills (finance, marketing,
management) that are valuable in many industries, even creative ones.

**Arguments for Switching to Graphic Design (Following Your Passion):**

*   **Increased Job Satisfaction:** Loving your work can significantly improve your overall
happiness and well-being. Passion often leads to greater motivation, productivity, and a stronger
sense of purpose.
*   **Greater Creativity and Fulfillment:** If you're a creative person, a creative field will allow
you to express yourself and use your talents in a way that a business job might not.
*   **Potential for Innovation:** Passionate individuals are often more innovative and driven to
excel in their field, which can lead to unexpected opportunities.
*   **Evolving Job Market:** The demand for skilled graphic designers is growing with the increasing
importance of visual communication in marketing, web design, and other areas.

**Considerations to Help You Decide:**

1.  **Self-Reflection:**
    *   **How strong is your passion for graphic design?** Is it a fleeting interest or a
deep-seated desire?
    *   **What are your strengths and weaknesses?** Are you naturally creative, visually oriented,
and detail-oriented? Are you comfortable with technology and software?
    *   **What are your long-term career goals?** Where do you see yourself in 5, 10, or 20 years?
    *   **What are your financial needs and priorities?** Are you willing to potentially earn less
in the short term for a more fulfilling career?

2.  **Research and Exploration:**
    *   **Research the job market for graphic designers.** What are the typical salaries, job
duties, and required skills in your area or in areas you'd be willing to relocate to?
    *   **Talk to graphic designers.** Ask them about their experiences, challenges, and rewards.
    *   **Explore freelance opportunities.** Try taking on small design projects to see if you enjoy
the work.
    *   **Look into graphic design programs and courses.** What are the curriculum, costs, and
career services offered?
    *   **Investigate companies that you would potentially want to work for.** Are these roles in
demand? What do they pay?

3.  **Compromise and Hybrid Options:**
    *   **Can you combine your business skills with your creative interests?** For example, you
could pursue a business degree with a minor in graphic design, or work in marketing or advertising
where you can utilize both skill sets.
    *   **Could you start a freelance graphic design business on the side while working in a more
traditional business role?** This allows you to explore your passion without completely abandoning
the security of a stable job.
    *   **Could you get the business degree and work for a couple of years to make your parents
happy and then consider going back to school for graphic design?**

4.  **Communication with Your Parents:**
    *   **Have an open and honest conversation with your parents.** Explain your passion for graphic
design and address their concerns about job security and financial stability.
    *   **Present them with research and data to support your case.** Show them job market trends,
salary expectations, and success stories of graphic designers.
    *   **Be willing to compromise.** Consider their perspective and explore options that might
satisfy both your desires and their concerns.

**Key Questions to Consider:**

*   **What's worse for you: regret or financial insecurity?** Would you regret not pursuing your
passion more, or would the financial insecurity of a less-traditional career be a constant source of
stress?
*   **Is the "stability" of a business degree guaranteed?** The job market is constantly changing.
There are no guarantees, and success in any field requires hard work, dedication, and continuous
learning.
*   **How much weight do you give to your parents' opinion?** Ultimately, it's *your* life and
*your* career. While their advice is valuable, the final decision should be yours.

**In Summary:**

There is no right answer, and it's important to do your homework and seriously consider your own
values, priorities, and skills. Sometimes following your passion takes hard work and hustle in order
to achieve financial security. Sometimes, the peace of mind that comes from the security of a good
paying job is worth sacrificing some passion for. Weigh your options carefully, talk to people in
both fields, and make a decision that you can live with and be proud of. Good luck!
